Is the hottest day of the year moving? A reproducible method for measuring seasonal timing in climate time series.

Methods demonstration on synthetic data. Each city carries a known, hidden seasonal shift, so the page can test whether the method recovers it. No result here is a real climate measurement. The same pipeline runs unchanged on reanalysis data such as ERA5.

Methodology

  1. Data. Daily 2 m air temperature per location, 1950–2020. In production: ERA5 reanalysis (Hersbach et al., 2020) through the Copernicus Climate Data Store, cross-checked against Berkeley Earth and GISTEMP.
  2. Preparation. Drop 29 February, average grid cells around each city, check for gaps and unit errors.
  3. Seasonal fit. For every year, fit T(d) = c + a·cos(2πd/365) + b·sin(2πd/365) by least squares. The peak day is atan2(b, a)·365/2π, the phase of the annual harmonic (Thomson, 1995; Stine et al., 2009).
  4. Trend and uncertainty. Regress peak day on year. Slope × 70 is the total shift. The 95% interval comes from the slope’s standard error. A robust alternative is the Theil–Sen slope (Sen, 1968) with a Mann–Kendall test (Mann, 1945).
  5. Validation. Inject a known shift plus noise, then check that the recovered shift falls inside its interval. This page runs that test live.

Tools

Used in this demo

  • JavaScript, no libraries
  • SVG charts
  • Least-squares fit and regression written from scratch
  • Seeded random generator, so results are reproducible

Recommended production stack

  • Python: xarray, NumPy, pandas, SciPy, statsmodels
  • cdsapi for ERA5 downloads
  • Cartopy or QGIS for maps
  • Tableau or Power BI for stakeholder dashboards fed by exported CSVs
  • Git for versioned, repeatable runs

Limitations and next steps

  • The first harmonic assumes a symmetric, sinusoidal season. It measures the timing of the annual peak, not when spring or summer begins.
  • Real data adds reanalysis inhomogeneities, grid-cell choice and natural variability, so intervals will be wider.
  • Next: replace synthetic series with ERA5 for 50 or more cities, add a global gridded map, and add a vegetation indicator (NDVI) as a second, independent measure of seasonal timing.
